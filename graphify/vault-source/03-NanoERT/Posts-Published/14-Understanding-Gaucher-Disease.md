# Understanding Gaucher Disease

Understanding Gaucher Disease: A Rare Condition That Speaks Volumes — and how we are advancing enzyme therapy for a smarter tomorrow.

Gaucher disease is a rare, inherited lysosomal storage disorder caused by mutations in the GBA gene, which impair the activity of the enzyme glucocerebrosidase (GCase). Without enough functional GCase, a lipid called glucocerebroside builds up inside macrophages — creating "Gaucher cells" that infiltrate critical organs and tissues, leading to cell death.

Three types: Type 1 (non-neuronopathic, most common — hepatosplenomegaly, anemia, thrombocytopenia, bone pain, osteonecrosis); Type 2 (acute neuronopathic — rare, severe infantile form with rapid neurodegeneration and early death); Type 3 (chronic neuronopathic — slower-progressing neurologic symptoms including oculomotor disturbances, seizures, cognitive decline).

GBA mutations are also the most significant genetic risk factor for Parkinson's disease, linking Gaucher to broader neurodegenerative pathways involving alpha-synuclein aggregation and lysosomal dysfunction.

Current treatments for Type 1: ERT (recombinant GCase, e.g. imiglucerase, velaglucerase alfa) reduces systemic symptoms but does not cross the blood-brain barrier; SRT (oral agents reducing glucocerebroside production) has significant side effects and varying efficacy. Challenges remain: frequent intravenous infusions, high cost, limited penetration in bone and CNS, residual disease burden, no disease-modifying effect on neurological forms.

At NanoERT Therapeutics, we're tackling these limitations head-on. Our proprietary technology conjugates a recombinant therapeutical enzyme to a biopolymer, engineered to: improve enzyme stability, extend half-life in circulation, enhance targeting of affected tissues (including bone), and deliver superior pharmacodynamic performance compared to standard ERT. We're currently in preclinical development, and the data is encouraging.

Our vision: rare diseases deserve first-in-class solutions, not afterthoughts. We're building a smarter future for enzyme therapy — one molecule at a time.
