# GBA CTSD Alpha-Synuclein Axis in PD

Among our key players, the enzymes GBA (beta-glucocerebrosidase) and CTSD (cathepsin D) have a critical partnership for alpha-synuclein lysosomal turnover — a self-amplifying feedback loop.

1. GBA <-> alpha-synuclein: GBA alteration is the first PD risk factor. Mutations in GBA reduce activity, causing substrate accumulation, lipid imbalance, alpha-synuclein accumulation and aggregation, which further reduces GBA activity.

2. CTSD <-> GBA <-> alpha-synuclein: CTSD degrades alpha-synuclein and cleaves prosaposin to saposins, which enhance GBA activity. Altered CTSD reduces synuclein degradation and reduces saposins, further reducing GBA activity and increasing synuclein aggregation.

So what's next? ERT of CTSD or GBA improves synucleinopathy via lysosomal function recovery.

Our mission is to recover lysosomal health — re-establishing enzymatic activity and intracellular balance — to address both rare lysosomal disorders and complex neurodegenerative diseases like PD. We believe targeting the lysosomal-synuclein axis represents one of the most promising paths towards disease-modifying therapies.
